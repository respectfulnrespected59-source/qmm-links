First, find the actual leak

Before you pay anybody a monthly fee, spend one week counting. Most shops we talk to are certain they need more leads, and most of them are wrong. They are getting enough enquiries. They are losing them in the gap between "message arrives" and "somebody answers."

There are only three places a two-person shop leaks work, and they need different fixes:

Leak 1 — the reply gap

A DM lands at 11:40am. You are inside a wheel arch with a machine polisher running. You see it at 2pm. By then they have messaged two other shops and booked whoever answered first. In local service work, speed of first reply is very close to the whole game, because the customer is usually contacting several businesses at once and treating the first useful answer as the default choice. Nothing on this page matters more than closing this gap, and it is also the cheapest one to close.

Leak 2 — the booking back-and-forth

"You open Saturday?" → "Yeah what time?" → "Around 10?" → "10 is gone, 1 ok?" → "Yeah" → "Cool, what car?" Six messages to book one job, times thirty jobs a month, is several hours of your life spent typing instead of detailing. A booking link collapses that to one message and a click.

Leak 3 — the review that never gets asked for

The customer is delighted. They drive off. Nobody ever asks them to say so in public. Two months later you are wondering why the shop across town outranks you on Google Maps despite doing worse work. They asked. You did not.

Do the arithmetic on your own numbers before you buy anything. Count your inbound enquiries for one week and count how many you replied to inside five minutes. If you get roughly forty enquiries a month, close half of them, and your average ticket is $150, then lifting close rate from 50% to 65% is about $900 a month. That is the number to hold every price on this page against. If your figures are much smaller than that, the honest answer is that you should fix this with free tools and your own discipline, not a subscription.

Three jobs, not one product

This is the part most "top 10" lists skip, and it is why people end up paying for three overlapping subscriptions. The tools below are not interchangeable. They do three different jobs, and almost none of them do all three well:

  • CATCH — respond automatically when a call or message arrives and you cannot get to it. Missed-call text-back, DM auto-replies, after-hours responses.
  • BOOK — let the customer put themselves in your calendar without a conversation. Self-serve scheduling, deposits, reminders.
  • ASK — systematically request a review after the job, and route unhappy customers to you privately instead of to Google.

Map your leak to the job, then buy the cheapest tool that does that job properly. Setmore and Square are BOOK. OpenPhone is CATCH. Podium is CATCH and ASK, which is why it costs what it costs. Jobber, Housecall Pro, Urable, and Mobile Tech RX are BOOK plus job management, with some CATCH bolted on. GoHighLevel is all three, aimed at people who configure software for a living.

The cheapest working combination for most two-person shops is a free BOOK tool, a CATCH auto-reply from your existing phone plan, and ASK done by hand in ninety seconds a day. That combination costs nothing, and we would rather you tried it first than bought something on our recommendation and resented it.

The ten, one at a time

1. Setmore — the honest floor

Free tier with unlimited appointments and a bookable page you can drop straight into an Instagram bio. Paid is roughly $12 per user/month billed monthly, closer to $5 annually. For a two-person shop that just needs customers to self-book instead of DMing "you open Saturday?", this is very often enough, and the free tier is not a crippled trial — people run real businesses on it.

Setup is genuinely about an hour: create services with durations and prices, set your working hours, paste the link in your bio and your Google Business Profile. The single highest-value ten minutes is getting your service durations honest, because a booking page that overbooks you is worse than no booking page.

Drawback: booking only. It will not answer a missed call or chase a review. You are solving one third of the problem, and the free tier's reminder options are limited.
Pick it if your bottleneck is purely scheduling back-and-forth.

2. Square Appointments — best if you already swipe cards

Free for a single user including booking and payments; Plus is around $49/month and Premium around $149. The advantage is not the feature list, it is that booking, deposits, and payouts live in one place, so you stop reconciling two systems at the end of the month. Taking a deposit at booking is also the most effective no-show cure we know of, and it is built in.

Drawback: card processing rates are higher on the free plan than on the paid ones, so "free" stops being free as volume climbs — at some monthly card volume the paid plan is genuinely cheaper. Run that crossover on your own numbers rather than trusting the label.
Pick it if you already take Square payments.

3. OpenPhone (now Quo) — the missed-call fix, alone

A proper business number with automatic text replies when you miss a call, around $15–25 per user/month. It does one job properly: the customer who rang while you were buffing gets a text back within seconds containing your booking link, instead of ringing your competitor. It also gets your work calls off your personal number, which matters more than people expect the first time they take a Sunday call about a $40 wash.

Drawback: not a booking system and not a CRM. Most shops pair it with something else on this list, which means two subscriptions and two logins.
Pick it if phone calls, not DMs, are where you bleed.

4. Mobile Tech RX — built for the trade

Estimating, invoicing, and workflow built specifically for detailing, PDR, and reconditioning, starting around $30/month, with real-world plans commonly landing in the $135–245/month range once you add what you actually need. VIN scanning and vehicle-specific estimating are the standouts, and they are a real time saver if you are quoting different makes all day.

Drawback: the entry price is not the price most shops end up paying. Budget for the tier you will land on, not the one on the banner — and if you are a two-person shop doing retail work rather than dealer volume, most of what you are paying for will sit unused.
Pick it if you do dealer recon volume.

5. Jobber — the one you grow into

General field-service software from about $39/month: quoting, scheduling, invoicing, automated reminders, and a customer portal. Not detailing-specific, which is a genuine downside — you will be configuring generic service templates rather than picking "ceramic coating" from a list — but it is mature, properly supported, and it does not fall over when you hire a third and fourth person.

Drawback: per-seat pricing compounds fast, and the cheapest tiers cap users hard, so the advertised entry price rarely survives contact with a growing team. For exactly two people it is more than you need today.
Pick it if you intend to be a five-person shop within a year and would rather not migrate.

6. Urable — the coating and PPF specialist

Detailing-native, from roughly $45/month, and notable for unlimited users on every plan — which quietly makes it one of the cheaper options for a two-person shop, because you are not paying twice for the second pair of hands. Built with ceramic coating and paint protection film work in mind, including vehicle visualisation and coating-specific job tracking.

Drawback: deeper than a two-person operation usually needs, with a learning curve to match. If your work is mostly interiors and maintenance washes, you are paying for depth you will not touch.
Pick it if coatings and PPF are your main money.

7. Housecall Pro — strong app, priced for crews

Around $59/month for one user and roughly $149/month for up to five, with additional users billed on top. The mobile app is the best-polished on this list, dispatching is strong, and the automated customer messaging is good out of the box rather than something you have to build.

Drawback: the jump from the single-user tier to the team tier is steep, and two people sits awkwardly between them — you are either paying for one seat and sharing a login, which gets messy, or paying team money for a two-person shop.
Pick it if you are mobile-first and already run more than one van.

8. GoHighLevel — powerful, and the wrong shape for you

Starts around $97/month, then $297 and $497, plus metered usage charges for SMS, email, and phone minutes on top. It genuinely can do everything on this page simultaneously — catch, book, ask, nurture, report — and for an agency running twenty client accounts it is excellent value.

Drawback: it is built for people who configure software for other people. A two-person shop will either lose a fortnight learning it or pay somebody to set it up, which is a developer in all but name. The usage fees also mean your bill moves around month to month, which is the last thing a small shop's cash flow needs.
Pick it if you are becoming an agency, not a detailer.

9. Podium — the review machine, at a price

Around $399/month, typically on an annual contract. Best in class at what it does: pulling every message channel into one inbox and systematically extracting Google reviews from happy customers. Reviews are the single biggest lever on local search visibility, and Podium pulls that lever harder and more consistently than anything else here. If your problem is that you are invisible on Maps despite doing excellent work, this is the tool built for that exact problem.

Drawback: roughly $4,800 a year. For a two-person shop that is a serious share of profit going to software, and the annual contract removes your escape route if it does not work out.
Pick it if you have multiple bays and the volume to justify it. If you are two people, this is not your tool yet — and we would rather say that than pretend everything on a comparison page is equally suitable.

A 30-day plan for a two-person shop

If you do nothing else on this page, do this. It costs nothing for the first three weeks on purpose, because you should know the size of your problem before you pay to solve it.

Week 1 — measure, buy nothing

Keep a note on your phone. Every inbound enquiry: where it came from, what time it landed, what time you replied, and whether it became a job. Seven days. This is dull and it is the only week that matters, because at the end of it you will know whether your problem is volume or response time, and those have completely different fixes.

Week 2 — close the reply gap (free)

Turn on the auto-reply your phone already has. Set Instagram and Facebook business account instant replies — both are free and most shops never switch them on. The message should do exactly three things: acknowledge, set expectation, give the next step. "Thanks for messaging [shop] — I'm on a car right now, I'll reply within two hours. Need a slot sooner? Book here: [link]." Then claim and complete your Google Business Profile if you have not: photos, hours, services, service area. It is free and it is the highest-return hour on this whole page.

Week 3 — kill the back-and-forth (free)

Set up Setmore free or Square Appointments free. Three or four services with honest durations and prices, your real working hours, and a deposit if no-shows hurt you. Put the link in your Instagram bio, your Google Business Profile, and your auto-reply. You have now solved BOOK and CATCH for zero pounds.

Week 4 — ask, and only now consider paying

Send a review request by hand the day after every job. One message, a direct link to your Google review page, ninety seconds a day. Then compare week 4's numbers to week 1's. If the free stack fixed it, stop here and keep your money. If you are still leaking — too many enquiries to answer by hand, review requests you keep forgetting, or you are ready to hire — now you know precisely which job to buy for, and the table above tells you the cheapest tool that does it.

Frequently asked questions

Do I actually need AI for a two-person detail shop?

Mostly no, and be suspicious of anyone who says otherwise. What you need is automation — something that replies when you cannot. Most of what is marketed as AI in this category is a scheduled auto-reply. That is genuinely valuable and it should not cost $300 a month.

What is the cheapest setup that actually works?

A free Setmore or Square booking page, a missed-call auto-text from your existing phone plan, a claimed Google Business Profile, and a review request you send by hand the day after the job. Zero dollars. If that is all you take from this page, it was worth writing.

Should I use an AI chatbot to answer Instagram DMs?

Use it to triage, not to close. An auto-reply that acknowledges the message, sets a response-time expectation, and hands over a booking link works well. A bot that tries to quote a ceramic coating without seeing the paint will quote wrong, and you will either eat the difference or start the relationship by revising the price upward. Detailing prices depend on condition, and condition needs eyes.

Does Instagram let me auto-reply to DMs for free?

Yes, if your account is set up as a business or creator account. Instant replies and saved quick replies are built in and cost nothing. A surprising number of shops pay for a third-party tool to do what the platform already does. Switch the free one on first and see whether you still need more.

What about Google Business Profile — is that a tool?

It is free, and for a local detail shop it is more important than any paid product on this list. Claimed profile, accurate hours, real photos of your own work, services listed, and a steady trickle of reviews. If you are choosing between spending an hour on your Google profile and an hour evaluating software, do the profile.

How long before it pays for itself?

One recovered job. If your average ticket is $150 and the tool costs $30/month, catching one missed enquiry a month covers it five times over. That is the honest math, and it is also why the expensive tools are not automatically wrong — they are wrong at your volume, and right at somebody else's.

Do I need a website?

Not first. A complete Google Business Profile plus a booking link will outperform a neglected website for local search. Build the site once those two are working and you have something to point it at.
