First, find where the money actually leaks

Before you pay anyone a monthly fee, spend one week counting. Most mobile mechanics we talk to are sure they need more leads. Most of them are wrong. The enquiries are arriving. They are dying in the gap between "phone buzzes" and "somebody answers," or on a driveway forty minutes away that was never going to become a paid job.

A mobile operation leaks in three specific places, and they need completely different fixes.

Leak 1 — the reply gap, which is worse for you than for a shop

A shop has a counter and somebody standing at it. You have a phone in your pocket and both hands in an engine bay. A call at 10:40 gets returned at 1:15, and by then they have rung two other mobile guys and booked whoever picked up. This matters more in mobile repair than in almost any other trade because a large share of your calls are urgent — the car will not start, it is making a noise, they need it for work tomorrow. Urgency does not wait for a callback. Closing this gap is the single highest-return thing on this page, and it is also the cheapest.

Leak 2 — the drive-out that was never a job

This is the leak that is specific to you and that no detailing or lawn-care guide will mention. You drive thirty-five minutes, diagnose for twenty, and the customer says they will "think about it." You have now spent an hour and a tank's worth of fuel earning nothing. Multiply by three a week and you are losing the better part of a working day, every week, for free.

The fix is not software. It is collecting a diagnostic fee at the moment of booking rather than on arrival. Every tool on this list that can take a deposit at scheduling solves this; the ones that only invoice after the work solve nothing. When you evaluate these options, that single capability matters more than every AI feature combined.

Leak 3 — the review that never gets asked for

You fixed someone's alternator in their own driveway on a Sunday. They were thrilled. Nobody ever asked them to say so in public. Two months later you are wondering how the guy across town outranks you in the map pack. He asked. You did not. For a business with no physical address, local search ranking is close to your entire marketing, and reviews are the biggest lever in it.

Do the arithmetic on your own numbers before buying anything. Count your inbound calls and texts for one week, and count how many you responded to within five minutes. If you take roughly twenty-five enquiries a month, convert half, and your average ticket is $280, then lifting your close rate from 50% to 65% is about $1,050 a month. Hold every price below against that figure. If your numbers are much smaller, the honest answer is that you should fix this with the free stack and your own discipline, not a subscription.

What we did not do: we did not run all ten in a live business for a season. Nobody publishing a list like this has. Where a tool is genuinely better than the alternatives for your situation, we say so, including when that means telling you not to buy anything at all.

On the phrase "AI tools." Most of what is sold as AI in this category is a scheduled auto-reply, a template, or a form that fills itself in. Some of it is genuinely useful. Very little of it is intelligent, and almost none of it should be allowed to quote a repair without you reading it first. We have flagged which "AI" features are real and which are marketing.

Three jobs, not one product

The single most expensive mistake in this category is buying one platform to do three unrelated jobs, because the platform that is excellent at one is usually mediocre at the others and priced for all three.

  • Job A — catch the enquiry. Missed-call text-back, an auto-reply that acknowledges and books, a number that does not ring out.
  • Job B — schedule it and take money up front. A booking link that shows your real availability and takes the diagnostic fee at the point of booking.
  • Job C — look legitimate and get found. Google Business Profile, review requests, invoices that do not look like a text message.

Job A is nearly free and returns the most. Job C is entirely free and returns the second most. Job B is where paying actually makes sense. Most of the platforms below are sold on Job B while quietly charging you for a Job A and C you could have had for nothing.

The ten, one at a time

1. The free stack — $0

A claimed and fully filled-in Google Business Profile with your service area and real photos, missed-call text-back (most mobile carriers and VoIP providers include some form of auto-reply free), and a review request you send by hand the day after every job.

This is not a consolation prize. For a solo mobile mechanic this combination beats roughly half the paid list, because it fixes Leak 1 and Leak 3 — the two biggest — at zero cost. The Google profile in particular is close to your entire local marketing when you have no shopfront.

Verdict: Start here. Always. If you have not done this, no subscription will save you, because you are paying to route traffic you have not earned yet.

2. Square Appointments — free tier for solo operators

Square publishes a free tier described for solo professionals taking payments and managing schedules, with paid Plus and Premium tiers above it. Booking link, calendar, card payments on site.

We could not verify Square's exact card-processing percentages: the pricing page renders those figures dynamically and they did not load for us. Confirm the current rate before you commit — we are not going to quote you a number we did not read.

Verdict: The cheapest legitimate way to solve Job B. If the free tier takes a deposit at booking, it closes your drive-out leak for nothing.

3. QuoteIQ — $29.99/mo Essentials (1 user, 500 AI credits)

Essentials $29.99/mo ($25/mo annually), Beginner $74.99, Pro $149.99, Elite $299, Max $699. Built for small field-service operators, with estimating, scheduling and invoicing, and it leans hard on its AI credit system.

Worth knowing: QuoteIQ also publishes a great deal of comparison content ranking software in this exact category. That does not make the product bad — the entry price is genuinely among the lowest here — but when you are researching, notice how much of what you are reading is written by a company selling one of the options.

Verdict: Reasonable if you want one cheap tool covering estimates and scheduling. Watch that the AI credit ceiling matches your actual volume.

4. Mobile Tech RX — $39/mo Getting Started

Getting Started $39/mo ($429/yr), Standard $99/mo, Pro $199/mo. Additional users $15–$29/mo. Add-ons: BlueDriver MAX $50/mo, Zapier $35/mo.

This is the one on the list actually built for mobile trades rather than adapted to them — estimating and invoicing designed to be done standing next to the vehicle, on a phone, with dirty hands. The entry tier includes one service module and 50 text alerts a month, which is a real constraint to check against your volume before you sign up.

Verdict: The strongest fit on this list for a genuine one-van operation. Priced like a tool rather than a platform.

5. Jobber — $49/mo Core (1 user)

Core $49/mo, or $39 on a one-year commitment, or $29/mo prepaid annually. Connect $139, Grow from $199, Plus from $499. Additional users $29/mo each. Add-ons priced separately: Marketing Suite $79/mo, Receptionist $29/mo, Pipeline $49/mo.

Jobber is general field-service software, not automotive-specific, and it is very good at Job B. The pricing is unusually honest — the tiers are published in full with the annual discounts shown — which is rarer in this category than it should be.

Verdict: Buy it for scheduling, quoting and getting paid. Do not buy the add-ons until the core has paid for itself.

6. Housecall Pro — $79/mo Basic (1 user)

Basic $79/mo ($59 annually, 1 user), Essentials $189/mo ($149 annually, 5 users), Max $329/mo ($299 annually, 8 users). Additional users $35/mo. Card processing from 2.59%. A long list of paid add-ons sits on top, including an AI receptionist.

Capable, popular in the trades, and noticeably more expensive than Jobber at the solo tier for a broadly similar job. The jump from Basic to Essentials is steep, and Basic is deliberately thin.

Verdict: Good software, wrong price point for one van. Revisit when you put a second tech in a second vehicle.

7. Workiz — pricing not published

Standard, Pro and Ultimate tiers are described on the pricing page, but no monthly figure is shown for any of them — every tier says "Request pricing." What the page does disclose is that additional users cost $55/mo (Standard) and $65/mo (Pro) on annual billing, and that the phone system and AI answering are sold separately on top.

Third-party sites quote wildly different figures for Workiz. We are not repeating them, because we could not verify any of them at the source and neither can you.

Verdict: A vendor that will not show a solo operator a price before a sales call has told you who the product is for. Not you, yet.

8. Tekmetric — $199/mo Start

Start $199/mo ($179 annually), Grow $349/mo, Scale $439/mo, Enterprise custom. Notably unlimited users on every tier, with no per-repair-order charge. Multi-shop $70/mo per extra location, Tire Suite $39/mo, Marketing $345/mo.

Genuinely well regarded shop-management software, and the unlimited-user policy is the most operator-friendly pricing model on this page. It is also built around a shop: bays, service writers, technicians, repair orders moving through stages.

Verdict: Excellent software, wrong shape. Unlimited users is worth nothing when the user count is one.

9. AutoLeap — $199/mo Essentials

Essentials $199/mo ($179 annually), Pro $349/mo, Elite $449/mo, Enterprise custom. AutoLeap AIR, the AI receptionist, is $99/mo on top. There is also an unspecified one-time setup fee covering data migration and training — the page confirms the fee exists but does not state the amount.

Strong digital vehicle inspection tooling, which is the feature most likely to matter to a mobile mechanic on this tier — photo and video approvals from the customer while you are still at the car close jobs that phone calls lose.

Verdict: The DVI is the real draw. Ask for the setup fee in writing before you sign anything, since they will not publish it.

10. Shopmonkey — $239/mo Basic (3 users)

Basic $239/mo ($215 annually, 3 users), Clever $399/mo, Genius $499/mo, Multi-Shop custom. Additional users $20/mo. Payment processing 2.9% + $0.30 online, 2.5–2.7% + $0.15 in person. Card readers $249 plus $10/mo per device.

Frequently ranked "best overall" in this category, and for a real shop that is defensible. For one person it is the clearest example of the mistake this whole page is about: the entry tier bundles three users because it assumes you have three.

Verdict: The most common wrong answer for a solo mobile mechanic. Right product, wrong reader.

A 30-day plan

Week 1 — count, do not buy Log every inbound enquiry: time in, time you replied, whether it booked. Log every drive-out that earned nothing. You now have the two numbers that decide everything below.
Week 2 — the free stack Claim and complete the Google Business Profile: service area, hours, real photos of your van and your work. Turn on missed-call text-back. Ask every customer from the last month for a review, by name, by text. Cost: nothing.
Week 3 — take money at booking Stand up one booking link that collects the diagnostic fee when the appointment is made. Square's free tier is the cheapest place to test whether this changes your no-show rate. It usually does, immediately and dramatically.
Week 4 — only now, consider paying Re-read your Week 1 numbers. If the reply gap is closed and the deposits are landing and you are still losing work to admin, buy the cheapest tool that fixes the specific remaining leak — most likely Mobile Tech RX or Jobber. If the numbers are already fixed, buy nothing and keep the money.

So which one, actually

If you are one person in one van: the free stack first, Square's free tier for deposits, and Mobile Tech RX at $39/mo when you outgrow that. It is the only tool here designed for the job you actually do, priced for the size you actually are.

If you want one general platform and dislike juggling apps: Jobber at $49/mo. Not automotive-specific, but honest pricing and genuinely good at scheduling, quoting and getting paid.

If you are about to put a second technician in a second van: this is the point where Tekmetric starts making sense, mostly because unlimited users on every tier means growth does not repeatedly re-price you.

If you are opening a physical shop with bays: then the ranking on every other site becomes correct and Shopmonkey, AutoLeap and Tekmetric are all serious options. That is a different article about a different business.

And if your Week 1 numbers came back small — under fifteen enquiries a month — buy nothing at all. Your constraint is demand, not admin, and no subscription on this page fixes demand. Spend the money on getting reviews and getting found instead.

Frequently asked questions

What software does a one-van mobile mechanic actually need?

A way to answer fast, a way to take a deposit, and a way to get paid on site. That is it. Everything else on a shop-management platform is built for a business with bays, a service writer, and technicians you dispatch — none of which you have.

Should I use an AI chatbot to quote repairs?

No. Use it to capture and triage, never to quote. A bot that quotes a brake job without knowing whether the rotors are scored will quote wrong, and you either eat the difference or have an argument in someone's driveway. Let it collect year, make, model, symptom and location. You price it.

Is shop management software worth it for a mobile mechanic?

Usually not at one van. Shopmonkey, AutoLeap and Tekmetric start at $199–$239 a month and are designed around bays, inventory and multiple technicians. Solo and mobile, you are paying for a building you do not have.

How do I stop wasting drive time on jobs that go nowhere?

Charge a diagnostic fee up front and collect it when the appointment is booked, not when you arrive. A booking tool that takes a deposit at scheduling kills tyre-kickers and no-shows better than any AI feature on this page.

Do I need a website for my mobile mechanic business?

Not first. A claimed Google Business Profile with your service area, photos and reviews will out-earn a neglected website for local search. Build the site after the profile and the booking link are working.
